================================================================================
VAANI QUALIFICATION RUBRIC
Aangan Studio - Pune / PCMC - as of 9 October 2026
================================================================================

Vaani forwards an enquiry to a designer only when all five of Nikhil's gates
pass. The rubric's working assumption is that a wrongly forwarded lead costs a
designer an hour, while a wrongly declined lead costs Rs 8-14 lakh - so every
gate resolves ambiguity by asking one question, not by guessing, and only a
clear two-gate failure ends the call.


================================================================================
1. THE FIVE GATES
================================================================================

The gates come from qualified.md unchanged. What this rubric adds is the
distinction between a gate that FAILED and a gate that is UNCLEAR - on a voice
call these look identical in the transcript, and treating them the same is what
loses a Rs 8-14 lakh lead.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
GATE 1 - REAL PROJECT
  Tests        Design AND execution, not advice
  Clear fail   "just looking for ideas" / "can you come and advise" /
               "I'll do the execution myself" / "just exploring"
  If unclear   Ask: "Would you want us to handle the execution as well, or are
               you looking for design only?"

GATE 2 - SERVICE AREA
  Tests        Site is in Pune city or PCMC
  Clear fail   Named locality outside the area - Talegaon, Lonavala, Nashik,
               Mumbai, any other city
  If unclear   Ask for the locality, not the city. "Pune" alone does not pass

GATE 3 - TIMELINE
  Tests        Site available for execution within 8-10 weeks of consultation
  Clear fail   Caller needs it complete sooner than execution allows
  If unclear   Ask: "When would you need the project complete?"

GATE 4 - BUDGET BAND
  Tests        No obvious misalignment
  Clear fail   A VOLUNTEERED figure clearly below any project of that scope
  If unclear   Never probe. Treat as pass

GATE 5 - DECISION-MAKER
  Tests        Caller decides, or is authorised and the decider will attend
  Clear fail   Caller is researching for someone who has not agreed to proceed
  If unclear   Never push. Treat as pass and note it
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------


1.1  WHY GATES 1, 2 AND 3 BEHAVE DIFFERENTLY FROM 4 AND 5

Gates 1-3 test facts Vaani can establish on the call in one question. Gates 4-5
test facts the caller may not want to discuss with an automated voice, and
pushing on either reads as pressure. So the rubric makes them asymmetric: 1-3
must be resolved before forwarding, 4-5 pass on silence.

This is a deliberate false-positive bias. Nikhil's own note settles it - the
designer establishes fit at the consultation, so Vaani's job is to catch obvious
misalignments, not to pre-qualify every variable a designer can handle in
person.


1.2  ABSENCE IS NOT EVIDENCE

Gate 4 can only fail on something the caller said. In September, one phone call
out of twenty volunteered a number that failed (T10, Rs 1-1.5 lakh for a kitchen
and bedroom). Fourteen others never mentioned budget at all. If Vaani treated
silence as a fail, it would have killed fourteen leads to catch one.

The same logic holds for gate 5. T14 - a son enquiring for his parents' 3BHK in
Hadapsar - reads as a fail on a first pass. It passes, because he confirmed the
parents would attend. Vaani's test is whether the decider will be in the room,
not whether they are on the phone.


1.3  TIMELINE FAILURE IS NOT THE SAME AS SCOPE FAILURE

A caller who fails gate 3 wants exactly what Aangan sells and is only early or
late. T07 asked for a living room and kitchen before Diwali, three weeks out - a
clear fail. The front desk offered a November start and the caller said they
would call back. That is a deferral, not a decline, and the rubric routes it
accordingly.

A caller who fails gate 1 or gate 2 cannot be rescued by waiting. Those are the
only two gates where a single clear failure ends the enquiry.


1.4  WHAT EXPLICITLY DOES NOT DISQUALIFY

Vaani must not treat any of these as a fail signal: not knowing what they want,
calling at 10:47pm, asking for pricing, being uncertain about materials or
layout, a single-room project with full execution, or a rented apartment with no
structural changes. Each of these appears in September's transcripts attached to
a lead the front desk booked.


================================================================================
2. FROM FIVE GATES TO ONE OUTCOME
================================================================================

2.1  BEFORE THE GATES: IS THIS AN ENQUIRY AT ALL?

Three September calls were not new enquiries and should never have entered the
rubric. Vaani checks for them first, because running an existing client through
a qualification script is worse than not answering.

  * EXISTING PROJECT, SOMETHING IS WRONG (T09 - designer unresponsive for five
    days). Interrupt immediately, take the name and project, promise a senior
    callback inside 15 minutes, hand to a human. No gates, no booking attempt.

  * CALLER HAS ENQUIRED BEFORE AND HEARD NOTHING (T16, W08). Apologise, skip
    straight to booking, flag the handoff note as a recovery. The gates still
    run silently but the tone changes - this caller is deciding whether to trust
    the studio at all.

  * CALL DROPS MID-SENTENCE (T17). On a redial within 10 minutes, resume rather
    than restart. Making a caller repeat themselves is the exact failure Nikhil
    is trying to remove.


2.2  THE COMBINATION RULE

  All five pass
      -> FORWARD. Book the consultation on the call, then send the handoff note.

  One of gates 1-3 unclear
      -> CLARIFY. Ask one direct question, then re-evaluate. Maximum two
         clarifying questions per call.

  Gate 3 is the only fail
      -> DEFER. Say honestly why the timeline does not work, offer the earliest
         date that does, capture details for re-contact.

  Exactly one of gates 1, 2, 4, 5 fails
      -> DECLINE. Close gracefully, state the reason plainly.

  Two or more gates fail
      -> DECLINE. Same, without working through each reason.

One clear failure on gate 1 or gate 2 is enough on its own. Scope and geography
are structural - Aangan does not do advisory work, and it has no vendor network
outside Pune and PCMC. No amount of passing on the other four gates changes
either.


2.3  WHY TWO QUESTIONS, NOT FIVE

The rubric caps clarification at two questions because of what Vaani is
replacing. The designer's first call is currently spent asking questions the
front desk already asked. A voice agent that interrogates a caller for five
minutes recreates that problem one step earlier.

Anything still unknown after two questions goes into the handoff note as an open
item. A designer resolves it in ten seconds at the consultation; Vaani cannot
resolve it at all without souring the call.


2.4  WHAT THE DESIGNER RECEIVES

Every forward produces one note. It exists so the designer never opens a call
cold:

  - Name, number, callback window, and how they found Aangan
  - Property: type, locality, carpet area, current state
  - Scope in the caller's own words
  - Timeline, with the date they stated
  - Gate results - five lines, each marked pass, pass-with-note, or unresolved
  - Anything a gate left open, phrased as the question the designer should ask
  - Full transcript, linked not pasted

The gate results are the part that matters. A designer who can see WHY this lead
reached them can tell in five seconds whether to prepare a site visit or a phone
call.


================================================================================
3. HARD RULES
================================================================================

These sit outside the gates. They apply on every call, including calls Vaani is
about to decline.


3.1  PRICING: NO NUMBER, EVER

Vaani never states a figure, a range, a per-square-foot rate, or a comparison to
a past project. Not when asked directly, not when pushed, not when the caller
offers a number first and asks only for confirmation.

The only permitted response is the one pricing.md specifies: pricing depends on
the site, the materials, and the scope; the designer walks through it at the
consultation, which Vaani can book now.

The reasoning is commercial, not legal. Residential specification runs from
Rs 1,800 to Rs 3,500+ per sq ft - roughly a 2x spread before site condition and
custom-furniture ratio are known. A number quoted on a cold call is wrong in one
of two directions: too low and the consultation becomes a renegotiation, too
high and the caller never books. The front desk held this line under direct
pressure twice in September (T02 and T13) and booked both callers anyway.

Asking for pricing is explicitly not a disqualifier. It is the single most
common thing callers ask for, and it correlates with intent rather than against
it.


3.2  THE THREE MOVES ON A PRICING QUESTION

A caller who asks about price gets a different move each time they ask. The same
sentence twice is what makes a voice agent sound like a recording, and a third
ask is usually frustration rather than curiosity.

  MOVE 1 - FIRST ASK: TURN IT AROUND.
  Vaani neither gives a number nor refuses outright. It says pricing follows the
  specification, then asks what range the caller has in mind for a project of
  this scope. This gets the budget signal gate 4 needs without probing for it,
  because the caller opened the subject.

  MOVE 2 - SECOND ASK: NAME THE REASON.
  The caller has heard the deflection and wants to know why. Vaani gives the
  actual constraint in concrete terms: the gap between a standard and a premium
  kitchen in the same flat is wide enough that a number over the phone would
  mislead them in one direction or the other.

  MOVE 3 - THIRD ASK: COMMIT TO WHEN THEY GET IT.
  Vaani states plainly that only the designer can price the project, that it
  needs the site and the specification in front of them, and that it happens at
  the consultation - then offers the earliest slot and books it.

What is fixed is the three moves, not the wording. Each line is rewritten for
the call in front of it: a caller who has already described a 950 sq ft full
redesign with a November move-in gets a sentence built from those details, while
a caller who has said only "roughly how much for a 2BHK" gets a shorter one,
because Vaani has nothing of theirs to refer back to yet. By the third ask the
sentence should be shorter than the first, not longer - a caller pushing for the
third time wants a commitment, not an explanation.

Vaani never recites the sentence in pricing.md verbatim. That sentence defines
what must be true of the answer: no number, the reason tied to site and scope,
and an immediate offer to book. Reading it out as written on every call is how a
scripted agent gets identified as one.


3.3  ASKING FOR BUDGET IS NOT PROBING

Move 1 sits close to a line Nikhil drew - his rubric says don't quote and don't
probe - so the boundary needs stating. Vaani asks about budget ONLY when the
caller raises price first, asks once, and accepts any answer including a refusal
to give one. It never opens the subject itself, never asks twice, and never
treats a non-answer as a gate 4 signal.

If the caller does name a figure, it enters gate 4 as a volunteered number,
which is the only kind gate 4 is allowed to act on. If they decline, gate 4
passes on silence exactly as it would have anyway. The ask costs nothing and
occasionally catches a T10 - the Rs 1-1.5 lakh caller - before a designer's hour
is spent on it.


3.4  SCOPE: DECLINE ON EVIDENCE, NOT ON GUESSWORK

Vaani declines a project type only when services.md names it: restaurants,
hotels, retail, gyms, architecture or structural work, standalone furniture
sourcing, standalone Vastu advisory, commercial above ~3,000 sq ft, and work
outside Pune and PCMC.

For anything services.md does not address, Vaani forwards with a note rather
than deciding. T18 - a 180 sq ft coworking pod - is the instructive case:
services.md states an upper bound on commercial projects but no lower one. The
front desk declined on judgement. Vaani does not have that judgement and should
not simulate it.


3.5  NEVER INVENT

Vaani does not offer a referral it cannot name, confirm a designer's
availability it has not checked, promise a callback time no human has committed
to, or describe past projects it cannot cite. On T19 the front desk was asked
for a referral to a hospitality studio and declined to invent one. That is the
correct behaviour, and it is worth stating explicitly because an unconstrained
voice model will produce a plausible name.


3.6  TONE WHEN DECLINING

Every decline states the reason. "We only work in Pune and PCMC - we don't have
the vendor network to execute elsewhere" is a better outcome than a vague
deferral, because the caller stops waiting. T10 ended with the caller thanking
the front desk for being straight about budget. That is the target for a
decline, not an apology.


================================================================================
4. DECISION FLOW
================================================================================

        Call answered, any hour
                  |
                  v
            New enquiry?  -------- no -------->  Escalate to a human
                  |
                  | yes
                  v
   +---->  GATE 1 - Real project  ------ fails -->  Decline - out of scope
   |       (wants execution, not advice)
   |              |
   |  re-check    |
   |              v
   +--  unclear   GATE 2 - Service area  --- fails -->  Decline - out of scope
   |  <--------   (Pune city or PCMC)
   |              |
   |   Ask one    |
   |   question   v
   +---->  GATE 3 - Timeline  --- timeline only -->  Defer, offer a later date
           (site ready in 8 to 10 weeks)
                  |
                  v
           GATES 4 AND 5  ---------- fails -------->  Decline - misaligned
           (budget band, decision-maker)
                  |
                  | all five pass
                  v
           BOOK, THEN FORWARD THE NOTE

The gates run in this order on purpose: gates 1 and 2 are the only ones that can
end a call on their own, so they resolve first and a doomed enquiry closes in
under three minutes. The clarify loop is capped at two passes, after which the
unresolved gate travels to the designer as an open question rather than holding
up the booking.

5.1  WHERE THE RUBRIC DISAGREES WITH THE FRONT DESK

T18, THE 180 SQ FT POD. The front desk declined on the judgement that the studio
is not set up for that scale. The rubric forwards it with a note, because
services.md caps commercial work at ~3,000 sq ft and sets no floor. The front
desk is probably right; the fix is to add a commercial minimum to services.md,
not to ask Vaani to infer one. Until that line exists, forwarding costs a
designer five minutes and declining may cost a project.

T13, THE AUNDH 3BHK. Timeline was never established - the caller asked about
cost twice and the conversation moved to booking without anyone asking when. The
rubric treats this as an unresolved gate 3 and forwards with an open question,
rather than assuming the silence was benign.


5.2  WHAT THE TABLE SHOWS

Twelve of twenty calls forward. Four decline, and every one of those four fails
on scope or geography - structural reasons, settled in under three minutes. One
defers. Three are not enquiries.

No call in September required more than two clarifying questions to resolve.
That is the evidence behind the two-question cap, and it should be re-checked
after the first month of live calls rather than assumed to hold.


5.3  THE GAP THIS RUBRIC CANNOT CLOSE

T08 is a missed call at 10:47pm with no voicemail, and a failed callback the
next morning. It is the only transcript in the phone section with no content at
all. It is also, in a sense, the whole case: ~33% of enquiries arrive outside
10am-7pm, and roughly half get no response within 48 hours. Vaani answering at
10:47pm converts T08 from a blank record into a scored lead. That is the single
largest source of value here, and it has nothing to do with how good the rubric
is.


================================================================================
6. OPEN QUESTIONS AND THE DELIBERATE GAP
================================================================================

6.1  THE ONE THING THIS RUBRIC REFUSES TO BUILD

Nikhil asked for three things. Vaani answers every enquiry in five minutes, day
or night - the rubric supports that. Qualified leads reach designers with
everything already asked - the handoff note supports that. The third is that the
system should quote standard per-square-foot pricing so people don't have to
wait for it.

This rubric does not do that, and the refusal is not a technical limit. Aangan's
own pricing guide opens by saying no number from it should be quoted to a
client, lists the four phrasings the agent must never use, and prescribes the
exact sentence to say instead. Nikhil wrote the rule that forbids what Nikhil
asked for.

The likely reading is that the frustration is real but misdiagnosed. What
callers want is not a number - it is to stop waiting. T02 and T13 both pushed
hard for a figure, were refused, and booked anyway, because the refusal came
with an immediate consultation slot. Speed, not a rate card, is what the pricing
question is actually asking for, and that is what Vaani delivers.

If Nikhil still wants a number on the call, the change belongs in pricing.md
first - a published, defensible band with the conditions it assumes. Until that
document says a number may be spoken, Vaani will not speak one.


6.2  STILL OPEN

  - NO COMMERCIAL FLOOR EXISTS. services.md caps office work at ~3,000 sq ft but
    sets no minimum, so T18 forwards when the front desk would decline. Needs a
    line in services.md, not a judgement call in the agent.

  - THE TWO-QUESTION CAP IS SET FROM TWENTY CALLS. No September call needed more
    than two. That is a small sample from a single month. Re-check after 200
    live calls.

  - LANGUAGE. W03 was conducted entirely in Hindi and converted. The phone line
    will carry Hindi and Marathi callers. Gate wording needs an equivalent in
    each, written by someone who speaks them - not translated by the agent at
    runtime.

  - CAN VAANI ACTUALLY BOOK AT 10:47PM? Forwarding depends on a real calendar
    slot being held. If designer availability is not writable out of hours, the
    outcome "book, then forward" degrades to "promise, then forward", which is a
    different and weaker product.

  - NO FAIL GETS LOGGED TODAY. Every decline should write a record with the gate
    that failed. Without it, there is no way to learn whether gate 2 is losing
    projects worth serving or gate 4 is firing on callers who would have paid.
